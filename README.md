## The meter

The meter under test is the composition-based analyser implemented in the
first cells of the notebook: an additive score over length and character
classes, an entropy term of `len(p) × log2(pool)`, and deductions for a
72-word dictionary, 11 literal keyboard patterns, numeric sequences, repeats
and a date-like tail. The score is clipped to `[0, 100]` and banded, with
*Moderate* beginning at 41 and *Strong* at 61. Table I of the paper states
the complete rule set; the notebook is that table in code.

It is our own implementation, written for an earlier project, not a deployed
third-party product. We study it because its rules can be stated completely,
which is what makes the component-level attribution in Section VI possible.

## Corpus provenance

All six corpora are the ranked dictionaries distributed with
[zxcvbn](https://github.com/dropbox/zxcvbn), which the paper uses as its
reference estimator. No corpus is collected, scraped or redistributed by us.

| Name in the notebook | Used in the paper as |
|---|---|
| `passwords` | the 30,000-entry ranked breach corpus — both the evaluation population and the "breach" corpus in the sweep |
| `english_wikipedia` | the encyclopaedia corpus the acceptable passwords are generated from |
| `female_names`, `male_names`, `surnames` | the "names" corpora |
| `us_tv_and_film` | included only in the all-six union |

The five dictionary configurations swept in Section IX are defined in
`CORPUS_SETS` in the notebook:

```python
CORPUS_SETS = [
    ("breach only",    ['passwords']),
    ("breach + names", ['passwords', 'female_names', 'male_names', 'surnames']),
    ("breach + wiki",  ['passwords', 'english_wikipedia']),
    ("wiki only",      ['english_wikipedia']),
    ("all six (used)", CORPORA),
]
```

Entries shorter than four characters are dropped; the notebook reports how
many (242 across the full union).

## The six populations

Four are drawn from the breach corpus: a 4,000-row sample of the whole list,
4,000 rows from the pool of entries of eight characters or more, and complete
enumerations of the 1,090 and 229 entries of ten and twelve characters or
more.

Two are synthetic and generated by the notebook:

* **policy-compliant mutations** (n = 2,000) — an `english_wikipedia` word of
  five to nine characters, capitalised, followed by a year in 1970–2025 and
  one symbol. This is the single template the paper's Threats section flags
  as a limitation.
* **random 16-character** (n = 2,000) — uniform over the printable pool.

All 21,076 candidate base words for the first are entries of
`english_wikipedia`, and none is an entry of `passwords`. That overlap is not
an accident of the design; it is the paper's central measurement, and
Section IX is about it.

## Determinism

No random number is drawn outside one generator seeded at 42 and consumed in
a fixed order; the notebook's final cell prints the seed along with the
package versions. Running the notebook top to bottom reproduces every number
in the paper exactly.

One version is not pinned to a number: the `zxcvbn` build used exposes no
`__version__`, so the notebook records it as `bundled`. The six ranked
dictionaries it ships are the corpora listed above.

## One difference between this notebook and the paper's figure

The paper's Fig. 1 carries two corrected labels: the first series reads
**original lists** where the notebook's plotting cell writes *published
lists*, and Rule 3 reads **coverage-discounted bonus** where that cell writes
*coverage-discounted entropy*. Both were renamed during camera-ready revision
so that the figure matches the text, Table V and Table VI, which all call
Rule 3 a bonus and the baseline the original list.

The figure stored in this notebook shows the earlier wording. **No plotted
value differs**: every point is a cell of Table V, and the labels are the only
change.
